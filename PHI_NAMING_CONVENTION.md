# Φ‑Naming Convention

In the Φ‑Network ecosystem, the placement of the Greek letter Φ relative to a term carries precise semantic meaning. This naming convention is not arbitrary; it reflects the causal direction, the dominant layer, and the type of interaction between the concept and the Φ field.

Understanding this convention is essential for navigating the architecture, because it tells you immediately what the component does, how it relates to Φ, and where it sits in the causal hierarchy.

---

## Something‑Φ (Postfix Φ)

When Φ appears after a term, the term is the active agent or source, and Φ is the target or medium being acted upon. The component generates, modulates, or writes into the Φ field.

The semantic pattern is:

**Something‑Φ means Something acts upon Φ.**

Examples include CodeGen‑Φ, which generates code that increases Φ. Sensor‑Φ is a sensor that measures and writes Φ data. Modulator‑Φ is a component that modulates the Φ field. DRE‑Φ is Dynamic Refractive Energy acting upon the Φ field. Harvester‑Φ is a device that extracts energy from the Φ field. Refractor‑Φ refracts the Φ field. Amplifier‑Φ amplifies Φ gradients. Detector‑Φ detects Φ fluctuations. Transmitter‑Φ transmits signals through Φ.

These components have an active role: the component is the cause, and Φ is the effect or medium. They are typically actuators, generators, modulators, and writers. They have a measurable impact on Φ values.

## Φ‑Something (Prefix Φ)

When Φ appears before a term, Φ is the source or origin, and the term is the manifestation or product. The component is derived from, powered by, or emerges from the Φ field.

The semantic pattern is:

**Φ‑Something means Φ acts upon Something.**

Examples include Φ‑GPT, which is a GPT model whose intelligence is grounded in and guided by Φ. Φ‑Resonance is a social network whose ranking emerges from Φ resonance. Φ‑Coin is a currency whose value is tied to Φ. Φ‑Compass is a browser extension that reveals the Φ field to users. Φ‑Fabric is hardware whose architecture is optimized for Φ computation. Φ‑Immortality is immortality based on Φ.

These components have a receptive role: the component is the effect, and Φ is the cause or source. They are typically products, platforms, manifestations, and services. They derive their properties from Φ.

## Something‑Φ‑Something (Infix Φ)

When Φ appears between two terms, it represents a bridge, coupling, or interface between them. The two terms are connected through the Φ field as a mediator.

The semantic pattern is:

**Something1‑Φ‑Something2 means Something1 and Something2 interact through Φ.**

Examples include Mind‑Φ‑Machine, which is a brain‑computer interface mediated by Φ. User‑Φ‑Content is the relationship between users and content as measured by Φ. Energy‑Φ‑Matter is the conversion of energy into matter through Φ. Time‑Φ‑Space is the coupling of time and space through Φ. Bio‑Φ‑Digital is the bridge between biological and digital systems via Φ. Phonon‑Φ‑Qubit is the coupling between phonons and qubits via Φ. Data‑Φ‑Energy is the conversion of data into energy through Φ. Light‑Φ‑Matter is the interaction between light and matter through Φ.

These components have a mediating role: Φ is the bridge or coupling. Both outer terms are agents, and Φ facilitates their interaction. They are typically interfaces, couplings, and relationships. They describe how two domains interact.

## Distinguishing the Three Patterns

### Direction of Causality

- For **Something‑Φ**, causality flows from Something to Φ.
- For **Φ‑Something**, causality flows from Φ to Something.
- For **Something‑Φ‑Something**, causality flows bidirectionally through Φ.

### Dominant Layer

- For **Something‑Φ**, the Something layer dominates and Φ is secondary.
- For **Φ‑Something**, the Φ layer dominates and Something is secondary.
- For **Something‑Φ‑Something**, both layers are co‑equal and Φ is the interface.

### Role of Φ

- For **Something‑Φ**, Φ is the medium being acted upon.
- For **Φ‑Something**, Φ is the source or origin.
- For **Something‑Φ‑Something**, Φ is the bridge or mediator.

### Typical Function

- For **Something‑Φ**, the typical functions are actuators, generators, modulators, and writers.
- For **Φ‑Something**, the typical functions are products, platforms, manifestations, and services.
- For **Something‑Φ‑Something**, the typical functions are interfaces, couplings, and relationships.

## Practical Rules for New Components

When naming a new component in the Φ‑Network, first identify the primary causal direction. Ask whether the component acts upon Φ, whether Φ acts upon the component, or whether both interact bidirectionally.

Then choose the pattern accordingly:
- If the component generates, modulates, or writes to Φ, use **Something‑Φ**.
- If the component emerges from, is powered by, or manifests Φ, use **Φ‑Something**.
- If the component bridges two domains through Φ, use **Something‑Φ‑Something**.

Be consistent across the ecosystem. Once a pattern is chosen for a component, maintain it throughout all documentation, code, and communication. Inconsistency creates confusion, which increases C and decreases K.

Use Greek Φ or Latin Phi consistently. Greek capital Φ is preferred for formal, mathematical, and technical contexts. Latin Phi is acceptable for plain text, code, and environments where Greek characters are problematic. Lowercase phi is used for variables and mathematical expressions. Greek lowercase φ is used for field values and densities.

Compound terms follow the dominant pattern. If a term already contains a Φ‑prefix or suffix and you need to extend it, keep the dominant pattern. For example, Φ‑GPT extended becomes Φ‑GPT‑Assistant, preserving the prefix pattern while adding a bridge. CodeGen‑Φ extended becomes CodeGen‑Φ‑Optimizer, preserving the postfix pattern while adding a bridge.
